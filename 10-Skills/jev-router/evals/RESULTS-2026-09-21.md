# Back-test: can Jev name the binding constraint? — 2026-09-21

Fourteen candidates whose verdicts TradeCC had already recorded, each with a
cause in `research/edge_inventory.py`. Two arms over identical cases, five
repeats each, 140 calls total. Decision rule fixed **before** running:

> adopt as a second opinion only if SUPPLIED ≥ 10/14 **and** SUPPLIED > STARVED.

| Arm | What Jev was given |
|---|---|
| **STARVED** | the candidate, plus the four cause names with the one-line descriptions the ledger itself carries |
| **SUPPLIED** | the same, plus the measured evidence behind each cause (the 9,982-hour enumeration figure, the 7.8489·(σ/μ)² floor, the LVR mechanism, the distinguishing feature of each) |

## Result

| | pooled over 5 runs | per-run range |
|---|---|---|
| STARVED | **44/70 (63%)** | 7–9 of 14 |
| SUPPLIED | **60/70 (86%)** | 12–13 of 14 |

Ranges do not overlap. Every one of the five runs passed the decision rule.
**Verdict: ADOPT as a second opinion.**

Median latency ~367 ms. Total cost for all 140 calls: **under $0.005.**

## The hypothesis held — and the detail matters more than the headline

The test existed because Jev had previously answered the copy-trading question
`LATENCY_RACE` three times at 0.76–0.84 when the recorded answer was
`DATA_INFRASTRUCTURE` at 0.02. The question was whether that was a hard task or
a starved prompt.

**It was a starved prompt.** Copy-trading is now 5/5 in both arms — supplying
the evidence fixed it and the case is no longer interesting. What is
interesting is the per-case table.

Evidence fixed five cases outright and **broke two**. The net gain is real and
the mechanism is not "more context is better".

## The two regressions are my fault, not the model's

**Capital-range increase: 5/5 → 0/5.** Under SUPPLIED it answers
`LATENCY_RACE`. Reading my own prompt back, the `LATENCY_RACE` evidence text
contains the phrase *"a $5-$10 retail trader on public RPC is structurally last
in queue"* — I put the position size inside the latency description. The
candidate is *about* position size. I built an attractor and then measured the
model falling into it.

**pump.fun graduation timing: 5/5 → 1/5.** The `DATA_INFRASTRUCTURE` text names
pump.fun explicitly, so this should have become trivial and instead got worse.
I do not have an explanation for this one. The pitch — *"trade the window
around graduation"* — may read as a timing race once the latency description is
made vivid. **Unresolved.**

Both regressions are prompt-design defects in the evidence text, and both are
fixable. Neither is a reason to distrust the answer; both are a reason to
treat the option descriptions as code that needs review, because that is what
they are.

## Calibration: correctly refuses to conclude

`calibration.py` returns **UNDERPOWERED** for both arms at n=14, because zero
bins reach the 97 samples needed to resolve a 10-point calibration error. That
is the right answer, not a failed experiment: accuracy at n=14 can show a gross
effect, and did, but it cannot certify that a stated 0.78 means 78%.

The directional signal is visible and should not be over-read: in SUPPLIED,
every case in the 0.5–1.0 bins resolved correct, and the single 0.2–0.3 case
resolved wrong. Consistent with calibration. **Not evidence of it.**

## One self-inconsistent response, then none

An earlier pass crashed on the argmax guard ported from `better-call-jev`:
Jev returned a `choice` that was not its own most probable option. It did not
recur — **0 of 140** in the clean run. Worth recording because the guard
earned itself on live data, and because a client that silently accepted that
answer would have acted on a choice the model's own distribution contradicted.

## What this does and does not license

**Licensed:** recording a Jev second opinion in the registry row beside the
human verdict on `repo-intake` step 2, with the evidence-supplied prompt.
Disagreement is the useful output.

**Not licensed:** letting it set a verdict. At 86% pooled, roughly one call in
seven is wrong, and the two regressions show the error mode is systematic
rather than random — it follows whatever the option text makes salient.

# Round 2 — the fix failed, and that is the finding (2026-09-21, later)

I predicted that removing the two contaminants I had found would keep the
gains and repair the regressions. **It did not.** Third arm, same 14 cases,
five repeats, 210 calls total:

| Arm | Option descriptions | Pooled |
|---|---|---|
| STARVED | names and a one-line gloss | 41/70 (59%) |
| **EVIDENCED (v1)** | plus specific measured facts | **60/70 (86%)** |
| MECHANISM (v2) | clean abstract definitions, no instances | 52/70 (74%) |

v2 scored **twelve points below** the version it was meant to improve,
repaired **neither** regression, and broke two more.

| Case | STARVED | EVIDENCED | MECHANISM | recorded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copy-trading | 5/5 | 5/5 | **2/5** | DATA_INFRASTRUCTURE |
| Statistical arbitrage | 0/5 | 5/5 | **0/5** | DETECTION_FLOOR |
| Capital-range increase | 5/5 | 0/5 | 0/5 | DETECTION_FLOOR |
| pump.fun graduation | 2/5 | 1/5 | 0/5 | DATA_INFRASTRUCTURE |
| Routing/fee-tier | 0/5 | 4/5 | 5/5 | LATENCY_RACE |

## What I got wrong

I called the named examples in v1 a defect — *"naming instances turns a
definition into a partial lookup table"* — and removed them. They were doing
real work. **Concrete instances act as few-shot anchors**, and stripping them
for clean mechanism definitions removed more signal than noise.

The instinct was to make the text read better to a human. It did read better.
It performed worse. That is the whole lesson, and no amount of reasoning about
the prompt would have produced it — only running it did.

## What survives of the original diagnosis

Two contaminants are still real, they were just not worth what removing the
examples cost:

- **A fact duplicated from `state` into one option.** `$5-$10` was in
  `state.trader_profile` *and* in one description; a candidate about position
  size was pulled toward it.
- **A named instance under a foreign centre of gravity.** pump.fun was named
  in a paragraph dominated by a different case, and scored 1/5 anyway.

## The two cases that never resolve, and a warning about the number

Capital-range and pump.fun are wrong in every evidenced arm. Reading them
again: **their recorded answers do not follow from the candidate description.**
One is labelled by project history, the other by a data-availability
measurement a reader of the pitch could not infer. That is a flaw in the eval,
not the model.

Excluding those two, EVIDENCED scores **59/60 (98%)**.

> **Do not quote that 98%.** The exclusion was chosen after seeing which cases
> failed — the cherry-picking TradeCC's backtesting protocol forbids by name.
> It is a hypothesis for a pre-registered re-run, not a result.

## Standing recommendation, unchanged

**EVIDENCED (v1) remains the prompt to use**, at 86%, as a second opinion
recorded beside a human verdict and never as the verdict. v2 is kept in the
file as the falsified alternative, because a failed prediction left in place
is worth more than a tidy history.

Self-inconsistent responses across all three arms: **0 of 210.**
